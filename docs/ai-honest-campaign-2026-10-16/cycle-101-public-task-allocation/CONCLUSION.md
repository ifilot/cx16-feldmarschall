# Public target allocation: original DOS screen

Reassigning a redundant capable pursuer did **not establish a DOS gain**. The
frozen candidate scored **9.84%** (16 wins, 16 draws, 212 losses; 12 unscored
errors), versus **9.80%** for Guard (15 wins, 18 draws, 212 losses; 11 errors).
Both arms earned 24 known points; the tiny difference between their official
percentages comes from different scored-game denominators.

Among the 117 side-swapped pairs complete in both arms, the paired difference
was **0.00 percentage points**, 95% interval **[−0.85, +1.07]**. Only three paired
scores differed: one improved, two worsened, 114 tied. This sparse screen does
not establish equivalence. All 128 planned pairs remain in the finite missing
outcome sensitivity analysis: candidate-minus-control bounds are **[−4.30,
+4.69] points**. Those bounds are not confidence intervals. Errors remain
unscored in official standings and their saved prefixes are retained.

The post-hoc [trajectory diagnostic](divergence.json) distinguishes behavior
changes from score changes. Of 244 matched games complete in both arms, 27 had
different move sequences; 24 of these retained the same outcome. Another game
diverged before a candidate-only engine error. Eleven games have identical error
prefixes in both arms. All 28 observed first different moves were by the
candidate at an identical preceding position. Later differences along those
trajectories are dependent consequences, not additional independent activations.

This was the first saved full-policy test of the unchanged previously registered
mechanism. It keeps each target's original primary attacker and redirects at
most one redundant nonspecialist to an uncovered disclosed target that it can
defeat. The multi-robot task-allocation literature motivates explicit capability
and task coverage; it does not predict that spreading Stratego attackers improves
game outcomes. The idea's separate honest development endpoint, cycle 102,
also established no gain. Neither endpoint justifies promotion.

The fixed study saved 512 attempts on matched original-DOS deployments and move
seeds, using rotated policy-owned armies. No gate, coefficient, stopping rule,
source or policy identity was tuned from these outcomes. Full replay and Qt6
review are retained in the reports and audit. Production remains unchanged.
