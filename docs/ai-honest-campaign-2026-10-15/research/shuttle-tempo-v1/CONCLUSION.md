Defer a shuttle-tempo policy. The legal forcing mechanism exists, but completed screening provides no demonstrated avoidable opportunity under the fixed public gates.

A two-square ban can temporarily forbid a disclosed defender's capture. In the constructed fixture, enemy Flag 0 lies behind known Bomb 10; disclosed General 30 has just shuttled from 20 with count five. Own Miner 21 can enter 20, which the General cannot capture immediately. Every legal enemy reply leaves a safe Bomb defuse on 10; after every next reply, the Miner can capture Flag 0. The own Flag remains screened, and an enemy Scout keeps alternative replies populated. At count four, 30→20 is legal and defeats the line. This is one temporary tempo, not permanent immunity: another unit's move resets the ban.

Native `threat()` and `flag_threats()` intentionally remain conservative about two-square restrictions. A legal-window mechanism would therefore differ from generic recurrence escape, which penalizes repeated positions; from task assignment, which diversifies existing goals; and from exchange extensions or escorts, which analyze captures or remove a defender. [Allis, van den Herik and Huntjens (AAAI 1993)](https://cdn.aaai.org/Symposia/Fall/1993/FS-93-02/FS93-02-001.pdf) motivates short forcing-threat analysis, without establishing Stratego guarantees.

The initial C21 native-template audit inspected all 85,971 own decisions. Its opponent never reached an active count-five shuttle. The expanded audit used only the explicitly allowed eleven native screening files from C19/C21/C22/C23: 4,400 games and 693,230 own decisions. It found 146 enemy active-ban roots, including 53 with disclosed stronger defenders, but no legal own move into the banned square which that defender would otherwise kill. Consequently there was no strict aligned Miner breakthrough. No validation cohort was read and the gate was not relaxed.

Own-side diagnostics found 165 selected quiet reversals reaching count five, six involving the last mobile piece. Five admit an exact public enemy waiting reply after which all legal friendly responses lose the last mobile piece and the opponent retains a legal move. This initially looked actionable. Checking every root alternative and both terminal objectives showed that all five positions were already publicly lost.

For example, C21 game 201, ply 318 has the sole own Marshal on 6, with shuttle count four. Reversing to 5 makes return to 6 illegal; disclosed Bombs on 4 and 15 then kill either legal reply after an enemy wait. However, the original alternatives also lose: 6→7 attacks a disclosed Bomb, and 6→16 allows the disclosed enemy Marshal 26 to trade, leaving our army immobile. Earlier entry into the pocket was an unknown capture, so it does not provide a strict public quiet-trap prevention certificate.

The other apparent escape, C19 game 140, ply 295, preserves the last Marshal's mobility but leaves own Flag 6 under the already disclosed Miner 5's immediate attack. Its alternative moves therefore do not save the position. The preliminary mobility-only classification is retained under `initial-mobility-only/`; the final analyzer explicitly tests public immediate Flag captures before calling an alternative unrefuted.

Six tests check the forcing fixture, count-four counterplay, rotation, hidden-nibble invariance, public Bomb requirements, exact all-friendly-response terminal trapping, already-forced alternatives, the Flag counterexample and aborting on uncertain combat. `expanded-result.json` preserves every certificate and source hash. These are correlated retrospective observations and bounded public proofs, not changed outcomes or strength results. No penalty, runtime module, registry entry or new match follows from this audit.
