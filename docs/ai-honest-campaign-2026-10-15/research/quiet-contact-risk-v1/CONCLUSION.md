The proposed quiet-move inference has enough observable opportunity to justify a separately reviewed pilot. This study does not show stronger play: it reconstructs one component of the native score on fixed public prefixes and never runs a candidate policy.

The frozen sample uses the first 16 saved games from the completed C21 template-prior screening arm, at own-decision indices 0, 20, 40, 60, 80, 100, 120 and 140. Short games receive no replacement prefixes. This gives 117 roots, 2,794 legal quiet moves and 230 captures. No validation cohort was inspected.

Replacing the native unmoved-piece multiplier of one half with the existing Q12 mobile probability changes quiet danger comparisons nonuniformly at 47 roots. Adding per-piece mobile capability from the same frozen template posterior changes comparisons beyond that occupancy control at 65 roots. For the danger component alone, occupancy changes a pairwise ordering at 25 roots, including strict reversals at 13; mobile capability adds ordering changes at 61 roots, including strict reversals at 34. Goals, advancement and other score terms could outweigh any of these changes. These counts do not describe changed final choices or independent games, and correlated prefixes cannot support a strength estimate.

The distinction matters. Occupancy uses the Flag/Bomb beliefs already supplied to the native engine, while mobile capability uses information the native capture bridge can exploit but native quiet danger currently discards. Both preserve the native maximum of contact losses. Neither changes aggregation to an expected maximum, adds search, assigns a generic information bonus or reads an actual concealed rank. Captures have exactly zero correction. The correction is the change in old-minus-new danger relative to native old-minus-new danger, with the source vacated before evaluating the destination.

The baseline reconstruction matches the actual compiled native static threat function in 2,880 checks covering both armies, all own mobile ranks and varied captured inventories. Additional checks cover concealed-nibble permutations, the known Scout ray exposed by vacating the source, integer rounding, channel isolation and unchanged captures. Four focused tests pass. Twelve sampled roots also give identical model marginals and corrections after changing every concealed rank nibble. The model receives its own starting formation and public moves/combat disclosures; the referee replay uses hidden ranks only to emit the same public combat facts and validate recorded moves.

The Python research calculation, including full rank marginals, existing Q12 rounding and all three danger channels for every legal quiet candidate, averages 1.75 ms per sampled root, with a maximum of 12.67 ms. This is an untargeted host measurement, not a CX16 fit or complete policy timing. The largest mobile-channel differences approach the Marshal's material value. A wrong template belief can therefore produce a substantial error; this should not be described as safe movement.

The prior is the unchanged approximate population model: 80% over the eight source templates and 20% uniform legal armies, conditioned on hard public movement and rank constraints. The uniform component approximates the actual procedural deployment branch. It does not model action-choice likelihood. The proposed max-risk bridge also discards correlations between contacts, retains native unknown equal-exchange full-loss treatment, ignores unknown long Scout rays, and ignores shuttle restrictions exactly as the existing threat function does. Small occupancy tails may round to zero in Q12. These are explicit approximations, not a Bayesian proof of optimal action choice.

Stankiewicz's [Opponent Modeling in Stratego (2009)](https://project.dke.maastrichtuniversity.nl/games/files/bsc/OMStratego.pdf) motivates per-piece Bayesian rank inference with inventory consistency. It does not establish this particular danger bridge or predict a strength gain. This paper was already part of the repository's research; the contribution here is a mechanically verified, isolated application and opportunity audit.

A possible future pilot should keep three immutable comparisons: existing native template policy; occupancy-only quiet correction; occupancy plus mobile-capability quiet correction. Preserve all captures, immediate terminal wins, tactical priority terms, score coefficients, goals, native persistent-state correction and one-search random-state behavior. Independently qualify the adapter before freezing any ID or running new matches. No runtime integration, registration, tuning or saved games were performed here. Full quiet corrections, posterior component probabilities and source hashes remain in result.json; source-inputs.zip preserves the mechanical oracle inputs.
