Defer a paired-Miner breach policy. The forcing mechanism is real in a constructed public position, but the strict public gate has no ready or nearby examples in completed C21 screening.

The idea differs from an escort. Two Miners prepare attacks on separate disclosed Bombs beside the same possible Flag. Defusing the first creates an immediate Flag threat, forcing the defender to remove that Miner. The second Miner then breaches another entry; a defender displaced onto the first entry cannot recapture it in one step. This is a forcing tempo sequence, rather than a bonus for keeping an escort near one lead Miner.

Across 85,971 own decisions in 400 completed C21 screening games, there were 720 target observations with two adjacent disclosed enemy Bombs. All targets were undisclosed possible Flags. Of these, 121 observations had two distinct Miners with traversable empty-transit routes to separate entries, but none had both Miners within three actions of their assigned Bombs. No ready pair or one-step preparation opportunity was found. The observations repeat positions within games and can include different candidate targets; they are not independent causal opportunities. An undisclosed target was never treated as a true Flag by the predicate.

The public toy fixture has enemy Flag 0, Bombs 1/10, a disclosed General 11 and own Miners 2/20. Own Flag 99 remains screened by Bombs 89/98; an enemy Scout 88 keeps nonterminal reply branches populated. After 2→1, every legal enemy reply either leaves 1→0 available or captures the first Miner with 11→1. In that capture branch, 20→10 leaves 10→0 available after every legal next reply. A separate equal-Miner defender fixture also removes the first defender. Three tests check this bounded mechanism, concealed-rank invariance, disclosure requirements and shuttle effects. The fixture demonstrates a tactical combination, not corpus coverage or playing strength; a publicly revealed live enemy Flag is itself atypical in terminal Classic play, while a hidden candidate gives only a conditional objective.

Source comparison confirms that `ai_mission.c` chooses one lead Miner, tracks one corridor/objective, clears a first obstruction and optionally removes a disclosed recapturer with another capable piece. It has no explicit paired-entry readiness or forced-recapture handoff. This establishes a distinct mechanism, but does not establish that native choices currently miss a usable instance.
