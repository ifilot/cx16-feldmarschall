A home interceptor sometimes needs to start moving before the existing two-turn Flag defense can help. This is a concrete prospective strategy, with retrospective opportunity evidence; it has not improved a policy or been tested in new matches.

Only the completed C21 screening cohort was inspected: 400 attempted games, 387 scored. Of 197 own Flag captures, 191 were by an enemy Miner. The audit uses actual event truth to replay the referee and label eventual outcomes. Its opportunity predicates immediately mask concealed enemy ranks and use only own pieces, public disclosures, occupancy and legal shuttle history.

The original idea of keeping a guard home whenever any disclosed Miner exists was too broad. It proposed thousands of changes even when the Miner was distant or had no traversable route. Some apparent guard departures were sensible escapes from disclosed attackers. We therefore examined a narrower scheduling mechanism: retain enough time to deploy a capable guard to the diagonal square shared by the two Bomb entries beside a corner Flag.

After a candidate quiet move, let the fastest disclosed Miner's current route need tau enemy actions to capture the Flag. Let a capable guard need d quiet moves to reach that post. The enemy moves next, so d deployment moves and one interception require d + 2 <= tau. All routes hold other pieces stationary, respect lakes and the current first-step two-square restriction, and avoid disclosed non-Miner attackers along guard routes. Own Miners count separately as guards which trade with the invader; stronger guards can survive. No route for the invader means no deployment deadline.

Across 7,842 eligible corner-screen roots with a disclosed Miner at least three actions away, 232 roots in 37 games had a selected quiet move which lost modeled deadline feasibility and a legal quiet alternative which retained it. All 232 were outside C10's selected-move demotion. Thirty-three of those games later lost the own Flag; the eventual captor was already disclosed at an opportunity in 28. These are repeated observations within games, not 232 independent chances to turn losses into wins. There is no counterfactual effect estimate.

For example, C21 game 3, ply 248 has own Flag 90, Bombs 80/91 and response post 81. Disclosed Miner 50 has a four-action route through 60, 70 and 80 to the Flag. Own Sergeant 62 needs three moves to post 81. The actual quiet move 31→21 spends the deployment turn; 62→61 or 62→72 preserves a two-move route after that action. This is a local scheduling opportunity long before the eventual outcome, not proof that the actual game would be saved.

The independent mechanical fixture is stronger but narrower. With Flag 0, Bombs 1/10, own guard 22 and disclosed Miner 3, moving the guard 22→23 allows the line 3→2→1→0 to capture the Flag despite all 49 possible pairs of intervening friendly replies. Moving 22→12 permits interception on 2 after 3→2. A Miner guard trades; a Sergeant survives. Ten tests cover this fixture, absence of an invasion route, existing C10 separation, rank distinctions, hidden-rank invariance and disclosed attackers.

This supports reviewing a persistent deployment role, rather than a score bonus or permanent garrison. It should leave captures, immediate wins and existing urgent defense intact, and fall back when a useful defensive alternative cannot be established. The current model is not sufficient to claim global safety: other pieces can move, concealed attackers remain uncertain, and a defensive diversion can lose an offensive race. One diagonal guard can stop either entry individually, but sequential two-Miner breaches can overload it after it captures the first invader. No formation change, runtime integration, registration or saved match has been performed.

The connection to [Alshiekh et al., Safe Reinforcement Learning via Shielding (AAAI 2018)](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/view/11797) is preservation of a modeled temporal response through action correction. Our stationary-piece approximation does not provide the paper's formal safety guarantees.
