# Cycle 44: public forks show no established honest-opponent gain

The public-forks policy scored 50.18% against the honest guarded student, compared with 49.73% for the unchanged template policy. Its paired difference was +0.45 percentage points, with a 95% interval [−0.18, +1.25] and a retained 98.3333% interval [−0.36, +1.43]. This exploratory cohort does not establish an improvement or policy equivalence. Only five of 280 pair score differences were nonzero: four positive and one negative, with exact two-sided sign p=0.375. The sign result diagnoses direction rather than formally testing the mean effect.

The strategic hypothesis was that a bounded public double threat, followed by a checked executable capture response, would convert games beyond the unchanged opening-inference policy. Each immutable arm played 560 Classic games across seven forced-army families, with 40 side-swapped pairs per family and matched physical armies and move seeds. The tactical helper uses public information only. This is an honest-reference movement comparison with supplied armies, rather than policy-owned deployment strength or validation against the original DOS AI. The source-template prior is deliberately tested outside its original deployment population here.

Fork results were 275 wins, 12 draws and 273 losses; the reference had 273 wins, 11 draws and 276 losses. All 1,120 games completed without errors and every planned pair enters the comparison. Missing-outcome bounds collapse to the observed +0.45-point cohort difference and are not confidence intervals. Sparse score-changing pairs constrain conclusions about rare tactical successes or failures; the near-zero estimate does not show that the policy makes identical decisions.

Recorded batch execution increased from 64.80 to 75.03 seconds, about 1.16 times as much. Different game lengths and concurrent load prevent treating that ratio as isolated per-decision proof cost. This is a desktop Python/native prototype; these results do not establish a CX16 port or target timing. No source, model, coefficient or immutable policy ID changed, and no tuning used this cohort. All games pass Python and native every-state replay, with two Qt captures and frozen runtime/source checks. Existing artwork and orientation are preserved. Production remains unchanged; no promotion follows.
