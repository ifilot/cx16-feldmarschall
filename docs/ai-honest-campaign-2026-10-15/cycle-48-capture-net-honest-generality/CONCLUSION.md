# Cycle 48: the public capture net adds no established honest-opponent gain

The bounded two-piece public capture net scored 48.39% against the honest guarded student, compared with 48.57% for the unchanged template policy. Its paired difference was −0.18 percentage points, with a 95% interval [−0.89, +0.54] and a retained 98.3333% interval [−1.07, +0.71]. This cohort does not establish an improvement. It also does not prove policy equivalence: 275 of 280 pair score differences were tied, with only two positive and three negative differences.

The strategic hypothesis was that a public two-piece capture net, with checked executable followthrough, would convert positions that the unchanged opening-inference policy missed. The immutable treatment and reference each played 560 Classic games across seven forced-army families, with 40 side-swapped pairs per family and matched physical armies and move seeds. Both use public information only; the tactical helper does not receive opponent setup or concealed ranks. This is an honest-reference movement test on supplied armies, rather than a deployment comparison or a DOS-strength validation.

Capture-net results were 266 wins, 10 draws and 284 losses; the reference had 267 wins, 10 draws and 283 losses. All 1,120 games completed without errors. Every planned pair enters the comparison, so missing-outcome bounds collapse to −0.18 points and are not confidence intervals. The exact two-sided sign p is 1 for the five non-tied pairs; this is a direction diagnostic, not a formal test of the mean effect. Sparse score-changing pairs limit what this cohort can establish about rare tactical benefits or failures.

Recorded batch execution increased from 68.73 to 117.39 seconds, about 1.71 times as much, without an established score gain. Different game lengths and concurrent load affect totals, so this is not isolated per-decision proof cost. The tested policy remains a desktop Python/native prototype; these games do not qualify target timing or a CX16 port. The source-template prior is also being evaluated outside its original DOS deployment population here. No source, model or immutable policy ID changed, and no tuning used this cohort. All games pass Python and native every-state replay, with two Qt captures and frozen source/runtime checks. Existing artwork and orientation are retained. Production remains unchanged and no promotion follows.
