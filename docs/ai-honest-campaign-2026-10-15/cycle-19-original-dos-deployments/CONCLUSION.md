# Cycle 19: validated deployment helps against original DOS armies

Validated own deployment improved both honest policies on this screening cohort, while the DOS opponent remained substantially stronger overall. The guarded student scored 9.14% with its protected random setup and 21.41% with validated deployment; the context teacher scored 6.82% and 14.19%, respectively. These results measure deployment plus play against armies generated by the original DOS level3 instructions. They do not reproduce the much higher scores obtained across the seven synthetic stress families.

The validated deployment mixture is already used by the CX16 game. Selecting it through the native tournament protocol repairs a benchmark mismatch in own setup; this experiment does not introduce a new production formation or move-policy improvement.

Each arm attempted 400 Classic games on 200 matched side-swapped pairs, with unchanged policy source snapshots, models and move seeds. Original DOS armies and their private setup seeds were matched across arms. Both armies rotate 180 degrees between sides, preserving the exact generated DOS-local formation. The explicit rotated mode was preregistered before any main games; the superseded plan and registration remain preserved under plan-revisions/v1.

Validated minus protected-random deployment improved student score by +13.32 percentage points on 152 complete pairs (95% paired bootstrap interval [+7.89, +18.59]; 98.3333% interval [+6.74, +19.74]). The teacher improvement was +6.12 points on 147 complete pairs (95% [+1.70, +10.71]; 98.3333% [+0.68, +11.73]). Pair directions were 65 positive/19 negative/68 tied for the student and 36/20/91 for the teacher; exact two-sided sign p values were 0.000000475 and 0.0440. These direction diagnostics are not tests of the mean effect, and screening across the campaign adds selection uncertainty.

There were 118 original DOS errors among 1,600 attempted games. Guarded student recorded 23 wins, 24 draws, 336 losses and 17 errors; validated student 58/39/265 and 38 errors; context teacher 20/12/349 and 19 errors; validated teacher 43/15/298 and 44 errors. Every error prefix is preserved and errors remain unscored in official standings. Assigning each missing outcome any score in [0,1] bounds the finite-cohort deployment effect at [+6.38, +20.13] points for the student and [+1.38, +17.13] for the teacher. These are missing-outcome bounds for this cohort, not confidence intervals; the higher error incidence in validated arms remains a limitation.

Recorded batch execution times were 230.32 seconds for guarded student, 260.68 for validated student, 454.76 for context teacher and 465.69 for validated teacher. Game lengths and concurrent load affect these totals, so they are not isolated per-decision costs. The deployment treatment adds no multiworld search to either underlying move policy.

The setup distribution is produced by unchanged original machine code, including the template and procedural branches, at level3. Frozen generator assets, emulator runtime, setup requests, pre/post RNG states and generated armies are preserved and reproducible. Private setup seeds are independent of the move seed visible to the honest policy. The move adapter separately reseeds the DOS move engine, whereas the original interactive game continues from the post-deployment RNG state. This is an original-deployment-distribution comparison, with that explicit RNG-continuation boundary; it is not a bit-exact reconstruction of the complete original UI game.

All 1,600 games pass Python and native every-state replay, with Qt review captures and independent frozen owner/original-generator setup audits. The guarded and validated move engines retain their registered information access; our policies receive no hidden enemy setup. The result supports further honest deployment research. No production policy is promoted, no source or model is changed, and fresh independent validation is required before a strength claim beyond this cohort.
